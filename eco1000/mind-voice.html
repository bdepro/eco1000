Mind Written Narrative

This part of the course asks one question: what does economic reasoning explain about something you actually care about? You build your answer as a personal narrative: a specific story from your own life, told carefully enough that the reasoning inside it becomes visible to someone else. The written narrative, the Indicator Analyses, and the viva conversation are graded together as a single Mind & Voice assignment worth 25% of your course grade.

Situation: At some point this semester a moment from your own life will click into focus differently than before — maybe as ordinary as a lease you signed without reading it closely — because you'll finally have language for what was happening in it. Go back to that moment and write down what actually happened, in enough detail that someone else could see it, before you reach for a concept to illustrate. We'll talk through the economic reasoning behind your story together in the viva.

Audience: Write for a reader who has never taken this course, someone who can see the scene and follow what happened before any economics enters the picture. Concrete detail carries the narrative. Follow Deirdre McCloskey's advice: speak to an audience of human beings.

Purpose: You're practicing something specific this semester: taking a moment you actually lived through and accounting for it with the reasoning you've built in this course. Notice honestly, and explain what you noticed in language someone else can follow. That honest accounting is the actual skill.

What Succeeds: A strong narrative shows a moment specific enough that the economic reasoning is visible inside it without being named. One that states the concept directly, or stays general enough to fit any story, is doing less than it could.

Scope: Everything happens in Eli Review — that's your workspace for thinking this through, not a place where you check off assignments one by one. Write, get feedback, rewrite, however many times that takes. What matters is the finished narrative: your moment, worked all the way through to the economic reasoning inside it. Submit that through Canvas once it's done.
